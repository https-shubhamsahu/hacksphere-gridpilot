## Why a hybrid
- The test window (mid-June to late July) is hotter than most training data. Trees cannot extrapolate: they predict a constant beyond the last split. The ridge base carries the hour-of-day profile and piecewise-linear cooling/heating terms, so it keeps rising with temperature.
- LightGBM on the ridge residuals picks up the non-linear interactions (hour x temperature x humidity x solar) that the linear base misses, with small trees and early stopping on the chronological block.
- Pure tree models (LightGBM, and by extension a tree-based QuickML pipeline) score about 15 kW RMSE on the hotter validation weeks with or without month/day_of_year, versus about 9.7 kW for the linear-base models. Calendar columns neither help nor badly hurt here; the gap is extrapolation.
- For QuickML: prefer a linear/GLM-style regressor (or an ensemble that includes one) on the quickml_train.csv columns, since the cooling_degree/heating_degree columns give it the same hinge-style temperature response.
